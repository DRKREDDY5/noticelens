# Understanding your CP01S notice

Source: https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp01s-notice

## What this notice is about

We received your identity theft claim. We'll send any refund you may be due after we finish working on your claim.

## What you need to know

- You can request an [Identity Protection Personal Identification Number (IP PIN)](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin) for added account protection by creating an account online.
- Learn how to protect yourself from identity theft and report it to law enforcement, credit bureaus, and financial institutions.
- Visit our [Identity Theft Central](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-central) to learn more about reporting identity theft to law enforcement. You can also find information on protecting yourself, and keeping your personal information safe, including your children’s taxpayer identification numbers, identity documents, benefits, and other identity information.

## Frequently asked questions

#### Who can I contact if I have more questions about my account related to identity theft? (updated Nov. 12, 2025)

You can contact us at [800-908-4490](tel:800-908-4490) for help with identity theft related issues.

#### Who can I contact for help with other tax account issues? (added Nov. 12, 2025)

You can visit our [Tools](https://www.irs.gov/help/tools) page or call the IRS help desk at [800-829-1040](tel:800-829-1040).

#### Where can I find more information on preventing identity theft? (updated Nov. 12, 2025)

You can find it by visiting:

- [Federal Trade Commission's (FTC)](https://consumer.ftc.gov/) for consumer information and their online [Privacy and Security](https://consumer.ftc.gov/identity-theft-and-online-security/online-privacy-and-security).
- [Social Security Administration (SSA)](https://www.ssa.gov/fraud/)
- [Taxes. Security. Together](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/taxes-security-together) for tips on keeping your personal, financial, and tax data safer.

## Tips for next year

- [File](https://www.irs.gov/filing) your tax return on time. If you can’t file on time, e-file [Form 4868](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-4868) to request an automatic extension of time to file. An extension to file a tax return doesn’t extend the time you have to pay.
- [Pay](https://www.irs.gov/payments) any taxes you owe by the due date to avoid interest and penalties, or explore alternate options like a [payment plan](https://www.irs.gov/payments/online-payment-agreement-application) if you can’t pay in full.
- Learn more about how to [get an identity protection PIN (IP PIN)](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin).

#### Need help?

- [You can authorize someone](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-2848) to represent you before the IRS or inspect and/or receive confidential tax information.
- You may be eligible for free help from the [Taxpayer Advocate Service (TAS)](https://www.taxpayeradvocate.irs.gov/contact-us/).
- You may qualify for help from a [Low Income Taxpayer Clinic](https://www.irs.gov/advocate/low-income-taxpayer-clinics).
- You can call the telephone number on your notice or letter. If you didn’t receive a letter or notice, use [Let us help you](https://www.irs.gov/help/let-us-help-you).
